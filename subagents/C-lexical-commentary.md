## Result: does the Greek of 1 Cor 11:2–16 require, permit, or say nothing about covering the FACE?

### BDAG (Bauer/Danker) — lexical data

- [EVIDENCE] **κατακαλύπτω** (BDAG 458): "cover, veil"; act. (Is 6:2) and pass. (Sus 32 Theod.); of a girl "κατακεκαλυμμένη ἕως τοῦ μετώπου covered or veiled to the forehead" (Hv 4, 2, 1) — down *to*, not over, the face.
- [EVIDENCE] At 1 Cor 11:6ab BDAG takes the form as middle, "cover oneself w. a veil, abs."; at 11:7 "W. acc. (either of the obj. or of specification, as Gen 38:15) τὴν κεφαλήν." BDAG's only object for Paul is τὴν κεφαλήν; πρόσωπον never appears.
- [EVIDENCE] BDAG's one classical parallel is Ps.-Dicaearchus on Theban women: "the covering of the clothes on the head is of such a kind that the whole face seems to be covered as with a mask" (458). A head-covering covering the whole face is attested usage — cited at 11:7.
- [EVIDENCE] **ἀκατακάλυπτος** (30): "uncovered"; ἀ. τῇ κεφαλῇ (Philo, *Spec. Leg.* 3.60) "with unc. head"; 11:5, 13 ("a woman without head-covering").
- [EVIDENCE] **κάλυμμα** (447): "head-covering, veil"; Moses' face-veil (Ex 34:33–35) at 2 Cor 3:13; "κ. is v.l. for ἐξουσία 1 Cor 11:10."
- [EVIDENCE] **περίβολαιον** (710): "'that which is thrown around': an article of apparel that covers much of the body, covering, wrap, cloak, robe"; 1 Cor 11:15 ἡ κόμη ἀντὶ περιβολαίου δέδοται.
- [EVIDENCE] **κεφαλή** (480): κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχειν "have (someth.) on the head" (11:4a; object left unexpressed); 11:4b listed under literal head, noting Murphy-O'Connor's "his head"="himself".
- [EVIDENCE] **ἐξουσία** §7 (311) is a whole note on 11:10: "Many now understand it as a means of exercising power … that is to say, the veil (κάλυμμα is v.l. for ἐ. here …)". BDAG itself glosses the word as the veil.

### Commentaries

- **Barrett** (249): covering "concealing the hair and upper part of the body, **not as a covering for the face**; so Schlatter." At 11:10 (253–254) follows Hooker: veil = sign of the ἐξουσία given to women; no face discussion.
- **Conzelmann** (183): "a man is not to have any covering on his head … while a woman is to have a covering." [INTERPRETATION] (187): "the rule that the head **or face** is not to be covered … the head, and more particularly the face, constitutes in a special way the archetype/image relationship." On ἐξουσία (188) he rejects Kittel's Aramaic etymology and concludes only that "the head covering represents a protective power."
- **Fee 1987** (506): κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων = "an external cloth covering," paralleled by Plutarch *Mor.* 200F; rejects "loosed hair" (507; 518 n.27). **Fee 2014** (583–584): περίβολαιον "ordinarily refers to a garment that functions as a shawl or 'wraparound'"; Murphy-O'Connor's "put-up hair" is "an otherwise unknown meaning." No face claim either way.
- **Fee 1987** (518 n.27) [DISPUTED]: "scholars assume that κάλυμμα ('veil') would be the proper term (this is found only as early as Epiphanius), when Paul himself uses περιβόλαιον ('shawl') in v. 15."
- **Thiselton** (828–829): the need is for "a head covering (Gk. ἀκατακάλυπτος …)"; "appropriate head covering (such as a hood)"; expressly postpones "whether ἀκατακάλυπτος may conceivably denote long hair that is 'loosed' down the back." No face.
- **Hays** (187): "The covering of the woman with bound-up hair appropriately symbolizes her relation to the man"; and "'authority' (exousia) does not mean 'veil' (as in the RSV)," over against the variant.
- **Héring** (106): Paul "specifies a veil which was to conceal **the hair and part of the face (at least the forehead)**." Note (109 n.31): iconography distinguishes "the long veil covering the face and part of the bust" (mourning/marriage/journeys) from "the short veil concealing the hair, the ears and the forehead," obligatory in good families. On the variant (108): Irenaeus κάλυμμα, Jerome *velamen*, Origen both ("velamen et potestatem").
- **Morris** (151): appearing "bareheaded was to act in what we would call a 'bare-faced' manner" — idiom for shamelessness, not cloth over the face.
- **Godet** (114): κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχειν = "having down from the head," i.e. "a kerchief in the form of a veil coming down from the head over the shoulders" — anti-face by description. On περίβολαιον (129): "does not merely denote, as κάλυμμα would do, an ornament for the head; it is a vestment enveloping the whole body, a sort of peplum."
- **Meyer** (248): "Having (something) down from the head, i.e. with a head covering. The Jewish men prayed with the head covered, nay, even with a veil (Tallith) before the face." Insists τὴν κεφαλήν = bodily head.
- **Edwards** (270–271) [pro-face, 19th c.]: "the injunction to men to pray uncovered and to women to pray with veiled faces is peculiar to the Christians"; "Women are enjoined to veil their faces."
- **Hodge** (209): two Greek types — the peplum "thrown over the head, and enveloped the whole person," and "the common eastern veil which **covered the face**, with the exception of the eyes."
- **Alford** (203): "woman ought to have power on her head (i.e. the sign of power or subjection: shewn by the context to mean a veil)."
- **Blomberg** (210): if cloth, "Paul probably wants married women to wear a shawl **over their hair and shoulders**"; elsewhere "whether veils, shawls, hats, or whatever" (indifferent).
- **Johnson** (189): the hair view holds Paul's "words … do not actually mention any cloth covering or veil (**whether covering the face or over the head**). Instead this sense is read into the texts."
- **Oxford Bible Commentary** (1125–1126): the himation "could be drawn forward from behind the neck to cover the crown of the head, or **even further forward over the face as well**"; "married women frequently covered their heads and even their faces."
- **Keener** (92): "Far to the east and in Arabia, modest wives might wear even **face veils** … in Jerusalem, Tarsus, and for some of the women of Corinth … it sufficed to cover the hair."
- **Ciampa/Rosner** (513): Paul covers "one's 'head' (the special concern is likely to be the hair) rather than one's 'face' (as in 2 Corinthians)." Explicit head-not-face.
- **Reasoner 2025** (443) [key]: "The last imperative of the verse, κατακαλυπτέσθω, **leaves open the question** of whether Paul has a head covering in mind or some sort of veil over the face." Also (435): κατά + gen. = "from," hence "something hanging from the head, an idiom for long hair"; Chrysostom glossed 11:4 with κομήν; Goodacre/T. Martin on περίβολαιον = "testicle" (435, 448).
- **Massey 2018** (503, 505): "Close attention to the original meaning of the words κατακαλύπτω … and κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων … permits a translation only of a material head covering"; "κατακαλύπτω is never used in ancient Greek to describe the covering of either a man's or woman's head with hair." n.12: "**Michael D. Goulder argues just the opposite: the veil or hood is drawn down over the face** for both males and females." n.13–14: Dionysius Hal. 15.9.2 (τὴν περιβολὴν κατὰ κεφαλῆς εἵλκυσε); Josephus *War* 7.128 ("most of his head").
- **Massey 2015**: κατακαλύπτω "describes veiling with a textile covering"; a woman "may wear her hair either up or down … and topped off with a veil" — the veil covers hair, not face.
- **Chadwick 2022** (7–8): Dio Chrysostom *Or.* 33.48 on Tarsus — women so arrayed "that nobody could see any part of them, **neither of the face** nor of the rest of the body"; yet "young unmarried girls were not expected to cover head or face."
- **Curtis** (8): the tegidion, "a rectangular cloth worn over the face with holes cut out for eyes"; Theban women "covering their whole face, with only the eyes showing"; Chalcedonians veiled half the face.
- **Humann 1981**: the next verse "speaks of an uncovered **head** (κεφαλή) not an uncovered **face** (πρόσωπον)"; assumes a headcloth "which covers the whole head and in particular conceals all the hair."
- **Hoelke 2014**: surveys the hairstyle thesis; reports Økland: covered "regardless of whether the covering is hair or a veil; it may very well be both."
- **Oster 1988**: local file is abstract/paywall only — no body text, no face data. **Peters 2025**: no face/head discussion found.

## Lexical summary

1. Every lemma is defined by "head," never "face": κατακαλύπτω "cover, veil"; ἀκατακάλυπτος "uncovered" (+ τῇ κεφαλῇ); κάλυμμα "head-covering"; περίβολαιον "wrap/cloak covering much of the body." No lexicon defines any of these as a face-veil.
2. Paul's expressed object is τὴν κεφαλήν (11:7); πρόσωπον never occurs in 11:2–16.
3. Ancient usage of κατακαλύπτω with the face does exist: BDAG's active example is Is 6:2, and it quotes Ps.-Dicaearchus where a head-covering covers "the whole face … as with a mask." So the verb can describe face-covering, but only where the face is stated or implied.
4. The 11:10 variant: BDAG, Héring, Alford, Fee, Hays all attest/assess κάλυμμα for ἐξουσία. It shows early readers took the thing on the head to be a material veil; it does not settle face vs. head, since κάλυμμα in 2 Cor 3:13 is Moses' face-veil while Philo uses it for a woman's headdress.
5. περίβολαιον denotes a body-wrap, not a face veil; the "hair-as-covering" reading is grammatical, not lexical, and BDAG/Fee treat it as a wrap.

- **Cloth, not hair:** Fee 506; Massey 503–505; Thiselton 828–829.

## Unresolved

- No lexicon or commentary in this corpus cites a NT-era Greek text using κατακαλύπτω + πρόσωπον for a woman's veil; the face-covering testimony is either later/other verbs (κάλυμμα, ἐγκαλύπτω) or non-Greek (Latin caput velare).
- Whether "head" is synecdoche for the person (BDAG notes Murphy-O'Connor; Meyer and Barrett argue literal) changes what "uncovered" means and thus whether the face is even at issue.
- The hair-vs-cloth question controls the outcome: on the hair reading the face question is moot; on the cloth reading the sources split between "hair/shoulders" and "face/forehead."
- Textual: Greek κάλυμμα at 11:10 appears late (Fee: "only as early as Epiphanius"); Latin velamen (Jerome, Irenaeus) may be translation, not a Greek variant — Héring and Fee read the evidence differently.
- Corpus gaps: Oster 1988 is abstract-only; Stanley and Peters yield nothing on the face; Massey 2015 and 2018 were sample-checked, not read in full.
