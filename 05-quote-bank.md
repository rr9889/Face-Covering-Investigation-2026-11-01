# 05 — Quote bank

Selected quotations, organised by topic. Every entry gives:

- the **quotation** (short, verbatim where verified);
- the **attribution** (author, work, date, page);
- the **corpus file** it came from;
- a **type tag** — `[E]` evidence (primary data: text, image, artefact, lexicon), `[I]` interpretation (a scholar's argument);
- a **verification mark** — ✅ checked directly against the source in this investigation; ◐ checked as reported by a second source; ○ secondary (not re-checked).

Quotations are deliberately kept short. Where a source's wording matters, the Greek is given.

## G. The article under examination

For comparison, the claims of *"Why Christian Head Covering Is Not Face Veiling"* (Head Covering Movement; David Phillips), and how they fared.

### Q64 — The article's core lexical claim
> "This word, by itself, does not refer to covering the face – or even covering the head."

- **Attribution:** David Phillips, "Why Christian Head Covering Is Not Face Veiling," Head Covering Movement.
- **Type:** `[I]` · **Verified:** n/a (assessment of the article).
- **Assessment:** **True** — and self-defeating. The same fact means the word does not *exclude* the face either.

### Q65 — The article's argument from silence
> "Paul could have specified that Christian women were to cover their faces, but he simply didn't."

- **Attribution:** ibid.
- **Assessment:** **The strongest argument in the debate.** Confirmed and strengthened by 2 Cor 3:13–18.

### Q66 — The article's use of Tertullian
> the Christian women covered their heads while the pagan women "cover not only the head, but also the face."

- **Attribution:** ibid., citing Tertullian, *Virg.* 17.
- **Assessment:** **Accurate as to the text**, but the inference is backwards: Tertullian is *urging* Christians to imitate the pagans, which shows that face-covering was not the Christian practice he was defending.

### Q67 — The article's conclusion
> "neither is face-veiling encouraged by Scripture, much less commanded."

- **Attribution:** ibid.
- **Assessment:** **Half right.** Not commanded: true. Not encouraged: overstated — the practice has real Christian warrant, just not Pauline warrant.
