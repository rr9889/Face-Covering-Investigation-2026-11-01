# 02 — Text and lexis

Everything in this file concerns the Greek text of 1 Corinthians 11:2–16 and its immediate lexical controls. Primary quotations are kept short; full references are in `05-quote-bank.md` and `09-bibliography-and-corpus-manifest.md`.

## 1. The passage (Greek text, 1 Cor 11:3–16)

Text as held in the corpus file `Native Greek.txt` (SBL-style text; public domain).

> 3 Θέλω δὲ ὑμᾶς εἰδέναι ὅτι παντὸς ἀνδρὸς ἡ κεφαλὴ ὁ Χριστός ἐστιν, κεφαλὴ δὲ γυναικὸς ὁ ἀνήρ, κεφαλὴ δὲ τοῦ Χριστοῦ ὁ θεός. 4 πᾶς ἀνὴρ προσευχόμενος ἢ προφητεύων κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων καταισχύνει τὴν κεφαλὴν αὐτοῦ. 5 πᾶσα δὲ γυνὴ προσευχομένη ἢ προφητεύουσα ἀκατακαλύπτῳ τῇ κεφαλῇ καταισχύνει τὴν κεφαλὴν αὐτῆς· ἓν γάρ ἐστιν καὶ τὸ αὐτὸ τῇ ἐξυρημένῃ. 6 εἰ γὰρ οὐ κατακαλύπτεται γυνή, καὶ κειράσθω· εἰ δὲ αἰσχρὸν γυναικὶ τὸ κείρασθαι ἢ ξυρᾶσθαι, κατακαλυπτέσθω. 7 Ἀνὴρ μὲν γὰρ οὐκ ὀφείλει κατακαλύπτεσθαι τὴν κεφαλὴν εἰκὼν καὶ δόξα θεοῦ ὑπάρχων· ἡ γυνὴ δὲ δόξα ἀνδρός ἐστιν. … 10 διὰ τοῦτο ὀφείλει ἡ γυνὴ ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς διὰ τοὺς ἀγγέλους. … 13 Ἐν ὑμῖν αὐτοῖς κρίνατε· πρέπον ἐστὶν γυναῖκα ἀκατακάλυπτον τῷ θεῷ προσεύχεσθαι; 14 οὐδὲ ἡ φύσις αὐτὴ διδάσκει ὑμᾶς ὅτι ἀνὴρ μὲν ἐὰν κομᾷ ἀτιμία αὐτῷ ἐστιν, 15 γυνὴ δὲ ἐὰν κομᾷ δόξα αὐτῇ ἐστιν; ὅτι ἡ κόμη ἀντὶ περιβολαίου δέδοται [αὐτῇ]. 16 Εἰ δέ τις δοκεῖ φιλόνεικος εἶναι, ἡμεῖς τοιαύτην συνήθειαν οὐκ ἔχομεν οὐδὲ αἱ ἐκκλησίαι τοῦ θεοῦ.

**Observation:** the lemma `πρόσωπον` does not occur anywhere in vv. 2–16. Neither does any synonym for face.

## 2. Lemma by lemma

### 2.1 `κατακαλύπτω` (katakaluptō)

**BDAG 458** (checked against the corpus BDAG extraction; direct quotations in quotation marks, paraphrase outside them):

> "cover, veil"; active in Isa 6:2 and passive in Sus 32 Theod.; of a young woman `κατακεκαλυμμένη ἕως τοῦ μετώπου` "covered or veiled to the forehead" (*Herm. Vis.* 4.2.1). At 1 Cor 11:6ab the form may be middle, "cover oneself w. a veil, abs."; at 11:7 "w. acc. (either of the obj. or of specification, as Gen 38:15) **τὴν κεφαλήν**."

BDAG then adds the crucial classical parallel:

> Ps.-Dicaearchus on the Theban women: the covering of the clothes on the head is "of such a kind that the whole face seems to be covered as with a mask" (BDAG 458).

**What this establishes:**

1. The verb is *generic* — it means "cover/veil," not "face-veil."
2. It can describe a covering that covers the whole face (the Theban example).
3. It can describe a covering that stops at the forehead (Hermas).
4. In Paul the only stated object is `τὴν κεφαλήν`.

**What it does not establish:** that Paul meant the face; that he excluded it.

### 2.2 `ἀκατακάλυπτος` (akatakaluptos)

**BDAG 30:** "uncovered"; `ἀ. τῇ κεφαλῇ` (Philo, *Spec. Leg.* 3.60) "with uncovered head"; 1 Cor 11:5, 13 "a woman without head-covering."

Note the dative of respect: "uncovered **as to the head**." The grammar itself names the head as the respect in which the woman is uncovered.

### 2.3 `κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων` (kata kephalēs echōn) — 11:4

**BDAG 480:** "have (someth.) on the head" (11:4a; object left unexpressed). BDAG lists 11:4b under the literal head, and notes Murphy-O'Connor's alternative ("his head" = "himself").

The phrase means "having [something] down over/from the head." Both Goulder and Massey agree on the syntax and disagree on the reference: Massey takes it as a material head-covering; Goulder takes it as a hood drawn down over the face (Massey 2018: 501, 504 n.12).

### 2.4 `περίβολαιον` (peribolaion) — 11:15

**BDAG 710:** "'that which is thrown around': an article of apparel that covers much of the body, covering, wrap, cloak, robe."

Godet's comment (1889: 129) is exact: it "does not merely denote, as `κάλυμμα` would do, an ornament for the head; it is a vestment enveloping the whole body, a sort of peplum."

So v. 15 compares hair to a *body-wrap*, not to a face-veil. It tells us nothing directly about the face; it does show Paul had a word for "wrap" available and did not use it for the cloth covering.

### 2.5 `κάλυμμα` (kalumma)

**BDAG 447:** "head-covering, veil"; used of Moses' face-veil (Exod 34:33–35) at 2 Cor 3:13; and "κ. is v.l. for ἐξουσία 1 Cor 11:10."

**The important point:** `κάλυμμα` is the word Paul uses for a *face*-veil — in 2 Corinthians, not in 1 Corinthians. Its semantic range covers both a woman's headdress (Philo) and Moses' face-veil (2 Cor 3:13). It is not a technical term for either.

### 2.6 `ἐξουσία` (exousia) — 11:10

**BDAG 311 (§7)** has a whole note on 11:10: many understand it as "a means of exercising power … that is to say, the veil (`κάλυμμα` is v.l. for `ἐ.` here)."

The word is not a garment term. The verse literally says the woman "ought to have authority on her head." Whether that means (a) a veil as the sign of authority, (b) her own authority to pray and prophesy, or (c) the man's authority over her, is disputed. **None of the three options concerns the face.**

## 3. Grammar and syntax

### 3.1 What is the object of `κατακαλύπτω`?

- 11:6a: absolute (`εἰ γὰρ οὐ κατακαλύπτεται γυνή`) — "if a woman does not cover herself."
- 11:6b: imperative (`κατακαλυπτέσθω`) — "let her cover herself."
- 11:7: explicit object (`τὴν κεφαλήν`) — "a man ought not to cover **the head**."

So the only explicit object in the passage is the head. The absolutes at 11:6 assume the object from 11:5–7.

### 3.2 `ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς` (11:10)

"Upon/on her head." The preposition is locative. It is not `περὶ τὸ πρόσωπον` ("around the face"). This is a small but real argument for the head-only reading: the verse locates the item *on the head*.

### 3.3 The force of `κατά`

- Nõmmik and Wu: `κατά` + genitive = "down" — a garment coming down from/over the head.
- Marlowe: the compound `κατα-` means "thoroughly covered," so `κατακαλύπτω` is a strong covering, not a token headband (Marlowe 2005: 10).

Both can be true at once: the prefix intensifies the covering and describes its direction. Neither fixes its lower limit.

### 3.4 `ἀντὶ περιβολαίου` (11:15)

`ἀντί` = "instead of / as." Hair is given "as a wrap." This is a comparison of function, not a definition of the cloth covering. The verse is often used on both sides of the hair-vs-cloth debate; it does not bear on the face.

## 4. The textual variant at 11:10

**NA/UBS text:** `ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς`.

**Variant:** `κάλυμμα` ("veil") instead of `ἐξουσίαν` ("authority").

| Witness type | Reading | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Early Greek MSS | `ἐξουσίαν` | No early Greek MS reads `κάλυμμα` (Nõmmik 2025: 300) |
| Irenaeus, *Haer.* 1.8.2 (Latin) | `velamen` | Quoted as "a woman ought to have a veil on her head" |
| "Hippolytus," *Comm. Dan.* (Greek) | `κάλυμμα` | Third-century citation, possibly Roman |
| Later Latin witnesses | `velamen` | |
| Bohairic | veil | |
| BDAG | lists `κάλυμμα` as a v.l. | s.v. `κάλυμμα` |

**Assessment:** the variant is secondary. Nõmmik's judgement is that it "does not appear to be a scribal 'corruption' … but seems to have circulated at first independently and outside of the Greek NT manuscripts until the Latin and Bohairic renditions of the NT incorporated it" (2025: 300–301).

**Why it matters:** the variant shows that from the late second century, readers took the thing on the head to be a material veil. Because `κάλυμμα` denotes an item whose purpose was head *and* face, that identification is the hinge on which the whole face-reading turns. Nõmmik: "beginning with the end of the second century CE, many interpreters of this passage focused their attention solely on the necessity for women to cover their heads and faces" (2025: 301).

## 5. The comparison passages

### 5.1 2 Corinthians 3:13–18 — Paul's own face-veil language

> 13 … Moses, who put a veil (`κάλυμμα`) over his face … 15 … whenever Moses is read, a veil (`κάλυμμα`) lies over their minds; 16 but when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed. 17 … 18 And all of us, with unveiled faces (`ἀνακεκαλυμμένῳ προσώπῳ`), seeing the glory of the Lord …

**Significance:** this is the decisive lexical comparison. Paul knows:

- the noun `κάλυμμα` for a face-veil;
- the verb `ἀνακαλύπτω` for unveiling;
- the noun `πρόσωπον` for the face.

He uses all three when he means the face. In 1 Cor 11 he uses none of them. This is the strongest single argument for the head-only reading, and it is why the argument from silence in this case carries weight.

### 5.2 LXX Genesis 38:15 — "she had covered her face"

Tamar: `ἐκάλυψεν γὰρ τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτῆς` — "for she had covered her face." BDAG cites Gen 38:15 at 1 Cor 11:7 as the parallel for the accusative of specification.

**Significance:** when the Greek wants to say "covered her face," it says `πρόσωπον`. The verb family does not do it by itself.

### 5.3 LXX Exodus 34:33–35 — Moses' `κάλυμμα`

The LXX uses `κάλυμμα` for the veil "upon his face" (`ἐπὶ τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ`), and the verb `περιτίθημι` for putting it on. Again: the face is specified.

### 5.4 LXX Isaiah 6:2 — the seraphim

`κατακαλύπτω` is used of the seraphim covering their faces (and feet). This is the one place where the verb itself takes "face" as its object — and it is the passage behind the "because of the angels" suggestion (see §6).

### 5.5 *Shepherd of Hermas*, Vision 4.2.1

> "…behold, there meeteth me a virgin arrayed as if she were going forth from a bridal-chamber all in white and with white sandals, veiled up to her forehead, and her head-covering consisted of a turban…"

`κατακεκαλυμμένη ἕως τοῦ μετώπου` — "veiled as far as the forehead." BDAG (458) and the `κατακάλυψις` entry (which glosses it "her head was covered with a snood," `μίτρα`) both take it as a head-covering.

**Assessment:** this is modest evidence for the head-only reading — the veil's lower limit is the brow, so the face is open. But it is a fictional allegory, and the phrase describes *this* woman's veil, not a rule. It was over-used in the popular article that prompted this investigation.

## 6. The two internal arguments for a face reading

### 6.1 "Because of the angels" (11:10)

Three main identifications of the angels:

1. **The Watchers** of Gen 6 / 1 Enoch, who lusted after women. On this reading the covering protects against angelic desire — which would give a functional reason to cover the face, though the text still says "head."
2. **Guardian angels** of the churches or of individuals (Matt 18:10), present in worship.
3. **The seraphim** of Isa 6:2, who cover their faces before God. Robertson and Plummer suggested this; it is the only reading that directly implies a face-covering, by imitation.

**Assessment:** the text says "because of the angels," not "like the angels." The inference is analogical, not textual. Fitzmyer doubts the Isa 6 reading. It gives the face reading a foothold, not a foundation.

### 6.2 "Head" as the whole person

`κεφαλή` can stand for the person (BDAG notes Murphy-O'Connor's "his head" = "himself" at 11:4). If "cover your head" means "cover yourself," the face is not excluded.

**Assessment:** the dress-level referent in this passage is unambiguously the physical head — it grows hair, it is shaved, it is anointed, the covering sits "upon" it. The synecdoche operates in the theological register ("dishonours his head"), not in the dress register.

## 7. The "head vs. face" distinction in the wider NT

The popular article cited four passages to show that "head" and "face" are distinct in Greek usage. They are, and the point stands:

| Passage | Head | Face |
|---|---|---|
| Matt 14:11 | John's head on a platter | — |
| Matt 6:17 | anoint the head | wash the face |
| Rom 12:20 | coals on the head | — |
| Rev 19:12 | crowns on the head | — |

**But the argument cuts less than it seems.** It shows that Greek *can* distinguish head and face; it does not show that a garment on the head cannot drape the face. The Theban parallel in BDAG is a head-covering that covers the whole face.

## 8. Summary of the lexical position

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Does `κατακαλύπτω` mean "face-veil"? | No — it means "cover, veil" | very high |
| Does `κατακαλύπτω` exclude the face? | No | very high |
| Does Paul ever mention the face in 1 Cor 11? | No | very high |
| Does Paul have the vocabulary for a face-veil? | Yes — and uses it in 2 Cor 3 | very high |
| Is the covering located on the head? | Yes — `ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς`, `τὴν κεφαλήν` | very high |
| Is the covering's lower limit specified? | No | very high |
| Does the 11:10 variant settle it? | No — it identifies a veil, not its extent | high |
